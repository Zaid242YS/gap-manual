1. What this app does

The Contractor Start-Gap Compass helps you act as a business evaluator when residential contractors begin work later than they promised. It does five things:

  • Explains why late starts happen and which workplace dynamics keep them going.
  • Measures real jobs you have observed: how often starts are late, by how many days, for which stated reasons, and at what cost to the client.
  • Scores a contractor or engagement process on six practices that prevent late starts.
  • Maps the industries that carry the financial cost of a late start.
  • Prescribes fixes through the Creed-to-Commerce™ Start-Gap framework, and produces a PDF report.

The key idea: a start date is a promise. The Start Gap is the distance between the date that wins the job and the date the business can truly deliver.

Before you beginThe app starts completely empty. It contains no sample data. Everything in your results comes from what you enter. It runs in your web browser and needs no account and no sign-in.
Not professional adviceThe app is an educational diagnostic. The Start Covenant it generates is a discussion template. Neither is legal, financial, or contractual advice; have a qualified local professional review any agreement before it is signed.

2. Quick start

If you only have ten minutes, follow this path.

  1. Open the app. Read Why it happens for two minutes to frame the problem.
  2. Go to Diagnose. Enter a job you have observed: contractor or trade, promised start, actual start (leave blank if not started), the excuse given, and your verdict on it. Click Add job. Repeat for each job.
  3. Go to Scorecard. Fill in the contractor name, then rate each practice from 1 to 5.
  4. Go to Industry impact. Tick the industries that apply, and optionally enter per-day costs in the calculator.
  5. Go to Framework & Fixes. Read the priority fixes the app drew from your scorecard.
  6. Go to PDF Report and click Save / Print PDF Report. Choose Save as PDF in the print dialog.
TipYour entries are saved automatically in your browser as you type or click. You do not need to press a save button. See Section 11 to export a backup.

4. Why it happens

This tab is reading material. It opens with the evaluator's read, then lists ten root causes as cards. Each card shows:

  • A who label: Contractor, Supply chain, Regulatory, Environment, Client, or System.
  • A foreseeability label: Foreseeable means the contractor could have anticipated it when promising the date; Partly means it is real but still plannable.
  • A short explanation and a Test it question you can ask on site or on the phone.

At the bottom, "The core insight" shows the self-reinforcing loop: an optimistic date wins the job, the pipeline fills, an earlier job overruns, the start slips, nothing happens to the contractor, and the same optimistic date is quoted again.

How to use itCarry the "Test it" questions into your conversations with clients and contractors. They convert a vague complaint ("he is always late") into evidence you can record.

5. Dynamics & Excuse Test

5.1 Workplace dynamics

Ten cards describe habits and incentives that sustain late starts, such as slot-hoarding, vague-date shelter, and blame diffusion. Each card gives What happens, Why it persists, and a Signal to look for, and carries a green tag naming the Creed-to-Commerce™ pillar that addresses it. Use the signals as an observation checklist.

5.2 The Excuse Test

The Excuse Test judges one excuse by the same five questions every time, so your verdicts stay consistent.

  1. Think of one excuse you were given.
  2. Answer each of the five Yes/No questions about it.
  3. When all five are answered, the verdict appears below the questions.
Yes answersVerdictMeaning
4 or 5LegitimateReasonable grounds. Record it, agree a revised date, treat as a one-off.
3Partly legitimatePart of the cause was real, part avoidable. Ask for the missing notice or mitigation.
0 to 2Weak excuseForeseeable or poorly handled; points to a planning or accountability gap.

Click Reset test to clear the answers and test a different excuse.

Good to knowThe Excuse Test is a stand-alone aid. It does not feed the Diagnose table or the PDF report. When you reach a verdict you want on record, enter it in the Your verdict on the excuse field when you log the job (Section 7).

6. Industry impact & calculator

6.1 Industries financially affected

Thirteen cards describe how a late start moves cost into other businesses, from homeowners and lenders to materials suppliers, insurers, and local government. The last card, small commercial fit-outs (cafés and retail), is marked adjacent because it falls just outside residential work but shares the same mechanism.

Tick the checkbox on each industry relevant to your assessment. Ticked industries appear in Section 4 of the PDF report. Untick to remove.

6.2 Cost-of-delay calculator

The calculator turns your own per-day figures into a total. Nothing is pre-filled.

  1. Choose a Currency (USD, SAR, MYR, AED, EUR, or GBP). The symbol applies everywhere money appears in the app and report.
  2. Enter the Days of delay.
  3. Enter any per-day amounts that apply: temporary housing or overlapping rent, storage and moving, loan interest or carrying cost, lost rental or business income, lost work time waiting at the site, and other.
  4. Read the three results: daily cost of delay, days of delay, and total client cost.

Two buttons sit below the results:

  • Use daily total in job form copies the daily cost into the Client cost per day of delay field on the Diagnose tab and takes you there. It requires at least one per-day figure.
  • Clear calculator empties the days and all per-day lines but keeps your chosen currency.

If the total is above zero, it appears in Section 5 of the PDF report.

7.3 The results panel

Once at least one job exists, the following appear above the table:

  • Five figures: jobs logged, percent late of jobs due, average days late, longest delay, and recorded client cost. "Jobs due" excludes jobs not yet due. "Recorded client cost" adds days late × cost per day across late and overdue jobs.
  • Evaluator's pattern read: plain-language findings generated from your data, for example whether late starts are the majority outcome, how many cited foreseeable causes, how your verdicts split, which pillar the most frequent excuse points to, and the total cost.
  • Excuses given (late jobs): a bar chart of how often each excuse was cited.
  • Job table: every job with status, days late, excuse, and verdict.

7.4 Removing and clearing

Click Remove at the end of a row to delete that job. Click Clear all jobs under the table to remove all jobs after a confirmation. There is no edit button: to correct a job, remove it and add it again.

Foreseeability behind the pattern readEach excuse category carries a built-in rating of whether it is normally foreseeable at signing: foreseeable (for example previous job overran, crew shortage, subcontractor no-show, cash flow, no reason given), partly foreseeable (materials, permits, weather, client-side, scope change, other), or genuinely unforeseeable (illness or emergency). The pattern read counts jobs by these ratings. They are general judgments, so always apply your own verdict for the specific case.

8. Scorecard

The scorecard measures how well a contractor, or the whole engagement process, has the practices in place that prevent late starts.

  1. Optionally fill in Contractor / process being assessed, Prepared by, and Project / site. These print in the report header.
  2. For each of the 18 practices, choose a rating from 1 to 5. Hover over a number to see its label.
  3. Read the Pillar results panel, which updates as you click.
RatingLabel
1Absent
2Weak
3Partial
4Mostly in place
5In place

There are three practices under each of the six pillars. The panel shows each pillar's average out of 5 and an overall score. Green bars are at 3 or above; red bars are below 3 and mark the biggest gaps. Practices you leave unrated are excluded from the averages, and a pillar with no ratings shows "not rated".

Click Reset scorecard to clear every rating after a confirmation. The three header fields are kept.

TipScore from evidence, not impression. For each item ask "Can I point to a document, message, or date that shows this?" If not, rate it low.

9. Framework & Fixes

9.1 The Creed-to-Commerce™ Start-Gap framework

The tab opens with the creed behind the framework: in Creed-to-Commerce™, commercial conduct flows from a creed and stays accountable to it, and a spoken start date is treated as a binding covenant. Six pillars then turn that creed into operating practice:

#PillarCore question
1Niyyah · IntentionCan the business honestly deliver this date?
2ʿAhd · CovenantIs the promise written, dated, and backed by a remedy?
3Bayān · ClarityAre scope, selections, permits, and responsibilities explicit?
4Iʿdād · ReadinessAre materials, crew, permits, and subs verified ready on schedule?
5Muḥāsabah · AccountabilityDoes one person own the start, and are slips reported early?
6Iṣlāḥ · RestorationIs the loss repaired and the cause fixed so it does not recur?

Each pillar has its own card with a purpose statement, Contractor fixes, Client fixes, and a Measure you can track over time.

9.2 Priority fixes from your scorecard

Once you have rated at least one practice, the gold panel lists the three lowest-scoring pillars with their contractor fixes, most urgent first. Before you rate anything it shows a prompt to complete the scorecard.

9.3 The 30-day rollout

A four-step schedule: days 1 to 7 capacity check and written start windows; days 8 to 14 lock scope, selections and permits; days 15 to 21 install the T-14 / T-7 / T-2 readiness gates; days 22 to 30 name a start owner, enforce the 24-hour late-notice rule, apply credits, and keep a slip log.

What T-14 / T-7 / T-2 meansReadiness checks held 14, 7, and 2 days before the start date: at T-14 materials and permits, at T-7 crew and subcontractors, at T-2 site access and the client.

9.4 The Start Covenant generator

This builds a one-page agreement from your entries.

  1. Fill in any of: contractor, client, project address, scope summary, promised start date, grace window (days), late-start credit per day, credit cap, and late-notice rule (hours).
  2. Tick each readiness item that has been confirmed: permit, materials ordered, crew named, subcontractor dates, client selections, site access, deposit purpose.
  3. The preview appears below and updates as you type. Unfilled values show as blank lines (__________), and unticked readiness items show as [ ] so they remain visible as open items.

The covenant covers promised start, readiness confirmations, written late notice, a per-day credit with cap, a release and refund option, record keeping, and signature lines. Click Clear covenant to empty all fields and ticks. Once any field or tick is filled, the covenant is added to the PDF report as Section 7.

CautionThe covenant is a template, not a legal contract. Local law governs what remedies and deposit terms are enforceable. Have it reviewed before use.

11. Your data: save, export, clear

11.1 Automatic saving

Everything you enter is saved automatically to your browser's local storage on this device. Close the tab and come back later, and your work is still there, provided you use the same browser and device.

11.2 Export and import

  • Export data (.json) downloads a backup file named start-gap-compass-data.json containing jobs, scores, ticks, calculator, covenant, excuse-test answers, and the header fields.
  • Import data opens a file picker. Choosing a backup file replaces all current data with the file's contents. If the file is not a valid backup, a message says it could not be read and nothing changes.

12. Assessing fairly

The app is built to identify patterns, but its conclusions are only as sound as the evidence you give it. A credible evaluator is consistent and even-handed.

  • Record the client side too. Late selections, blocked access, and late deposits are real causes. Log them honestly with the "Client-side" excuse; it makes your findings about contractors more credible.
  • Judge the same way every time. Run each excuse through the Excuse Test before assigning a verdict, and write the evidence in the Notes field.
  • Look for repetition. One late start may be bad luck. The same contractor, trade, or excuse across several jobs is a system problem.
  • Distinguish "valid" from "avoidable". A real supplier shortage can be a true cause and still be a planning failure if the order was placed late.
  • Focus on the fix. Use the gap ranking to open a calm, factual conversation about the specific practices that are missing.

14. FAQ

Does the app contain sample data?No. It opens empty by design. The worked example in this manual is illustrative only and is not loaded into the app.
Can I use it for commercial or non-residential jobs?The logic works for any contractor start. The guidance, cost lines, and industry cards are written for residential work, with one adjacent card for small commercial fit-outs.
Can I assess more than one contractor at a time?The job log holds many jobs from many contractors. The scorecard and report header describe one subject at a time, so for separate reports, export your data, clear, and start a fresh assessment for the next subject.
Can I edit a job after adding it?Not directly. Remove it and add it again with the corrected details.
Why is "Job value" not in any total?It is held as a reference for your records. The app measures delay and its cost to the client, not contract size.
Will two people see the same data?No. Data is stored per browser and per device. To share, send the exported .json file; the other person uses Import data.
Does it work offline?Once loaded, the app itself works without a connection. Only the Amiri font needs the internet; without it a standard font is used.
What do the Arabic pillar names mean?See Section 15. They are the Creed-to-Commerce™ expressions of the six practices.

15. Glossary

Start Gap
The distance between the start date that won the job and the date the contractor can actually deliver.
Promised start
The date the contractor committed to, ideally in writing.
Grace window
Days after the promised start during which no credit accrues.
Late-start credit
A per-day amount the contractor credits the client when work has not begun after the grace window.
Foreseeable
Reasonably predictable at the time the date was promised.
Slot-hoarding
Accepting deposits to hold a place in line without matching capacity.
T-14 / T-7 / T-2
Readiness checkpoints 14, 7, and 2 days before the start.
Pillar
One of the six practice areas of the Creed-to-Commerce™ Start-Gap framework.
Niyyah
Intention: sincerity about what can really be delivered.
ʿAhd
Covenant: a binding, written promise with a remedy.
Bayān
Clarity: explicit, dated scope and responsibilities.
Iʿdād
Readiness: verified preparation before the start.
Muḥāsabah
Accountability: a named owner, early notice, and records.
Iṣlāḥ
Restoration: repairing the loss and correcting the cause.
